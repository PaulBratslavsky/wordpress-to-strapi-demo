Accessibility audits can feel overwhelming. These five fixes cover most of the problems we find on small business sites, and each one takes less than a day.

1. Give every image a real description

Screen readers read the alt attribute aloud. "IMG_4032.jpg" helps nobody. Describe what the image shows and why it's there. Decorative images should have an empty alt="" so they're skipped.

2. Check your colour contrast

Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background, and large headings at least 3:1.

3. Make sure the keyboard works

{{image:laptop-flowers|Testing a booking form with the keyboard only. If you can't complete it without a mouse, neither can some of your customers.}}
  • Press Tab through the whole page. Can you reach every link and button?
  • Can you always see where the focus is?
    • If not, remove any outline: none without a replacement style.
  • Can you close menus and pop-ups with Esc?

4. Label your form fields

Placeholder text disappears as soon as someone types. Use a visible label connected to the field:

<label for="email">Email address</label>
<input id="email" type="email" autocomplete="email">

5. Use headings in order

One h1 per page, then h2 for sections and h3 inside them. Many screen reader users jump between headings to find what they need, so skipping levels leaves gaps.

Common questions

Do I need an accessibility plugin?

Usually not. Overlay plugins don't fix the underlying problems, and some make things worse. Fixing the markup is the reliable approach.

How often should we test?

Check new templates when they're built, and run a full review once a year.
