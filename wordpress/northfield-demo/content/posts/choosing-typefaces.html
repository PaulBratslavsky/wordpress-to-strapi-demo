A typeface that looks elegant in a brand book can fall apart on a cheap phone in bright sunlight. These are the checks we run before recommending a font to a client.

{{image:type-drawers|Old letterpress drawers at a print shop we visit for inspiration. Every drawer is a different size of the same face.}}

Our checklist

  • Readable at 16px on a low-resolution screen
  • Distinct characters: can you tell Il1 and O0 apart?
  • Enough weights for headings, body text and buttons
  • Language support for accents like é, ñ and ø
  • A licence that covers web, apps and print

Typography is what language looks like.

Ellen Lupton

Pairing two fonts

Two families is almost always enough: one with personality for headings, one that stays out of the way for body text. If you can't explain why you need a third, you don't.

We applied these rules on the {{link:portfolio_item:maple-street-bakery|Maple Street Bakery rebrand}}, where the menu board and the website share one type system.
